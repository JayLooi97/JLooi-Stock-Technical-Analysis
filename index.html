Methodology & how to read the scores

Pullback screen — three separate numbers, not one. Earlier versions of this tool collapsed everything into a single "Pullback %" that actually measured peak→trough, even after a stock had already bounced back substantially. That's now split into three: Max Pullback % (6M High − Swing Low) / 6M High is the deepest decline seen after the high, regardless of where price sits now. Cur DD % (6M High − Price) / 6M High is how far below the high price actually is right now. Off Low (Price − Swing Low) / Swing Low is how much of that decline has already been recovered. A stock can show a 35% max pullback, a 15% current drawdown, and a 31% recovery off the low all at once — three different facts, not one. Every stock is now sorted into a depth band based on Cur DD % — where price sits below the high right now, not the deepest point it ever reached on the way down: 5–10% (shallow), 10–15% (moderate), 15–30% (deep correction), or 30%+ (severe — see the Deep-Pullback flag below). Max Pullback % is still shown as its own column for comparison, but no longer drives the band. Use the "Depth" filter to focus on one band, or "any pullback band" to see all four at once. Anything under 5% isn't flagged at all — too shallow to call a real pullback.

Renewed momentum. A row counts as a live "setup" when it's in any of the four bands and 20-day trend = Up and price is already off the swing low. That combination is what "pullback followed by renewed upward momentum" means mechanically here — a shallow 5-10% setup and a deep 30%+ setup get the same badge, but the Setup Quality score below will usually rate them differently.

Technical Confirmation (new). This is a separate diagnostic layer built entirely from the existing GOOGLEFINANCE OHLCV history. It combines EMA 8/21 stacking, ADX(14) with +DI/−DI, MACD(12,26,9), 20-day ROC, Bollinger-band position/width, relative volume versus the prior 20 sessions, and recent 40-day price structure. It is not blended into Setup Quality yet: first we should collect enough historical observations to test which signals actually improve 5D/10D/21D outcomes rather than hand-tuning another arbitrary weight.

Trend Continuation score (new). Setup Quality and Test-Support only mean something for a stock that's actually pulled back — a leader that just keeps grinding to new highs without a real dip will score low or blank there, which isn't the screener being wrong, it's the wrong lens for that stock. This score answers a different question: is this still a strong, intact uptrend worth following, independent of whether it dipped? It's a weighted blend of recent price structure (40%), relative strength vs. the S&P — percentile if today's screen has SPY data, else a squashed raw gap (20%), long-term MA trend (10%), ADX/DI strength and direction (10%, heavily discounted if ADX is strong but pointing down), EMA 8/21 stack (10%), and MACD direction plus a small bonus for a fresh bullish cross (10%). Missing components are skipped and the rest reweighted rather than failing the whole score. (Re-weighted from an earlier 25/20/15/20/10/10 split after backtesting on this score's own intended population — uptrend, little/no pullback yet — showed price structure and relative strength carrying more real signal than long-term MA/ADX/EMA.) A row is flagged "trend" when 20-day trend is up, the score is 65+, and there's little or no pullback yet (this is the CRDO/MRVL/MU-style case — trending leaders the pullback framework can't flag by design). It's separately flagged "extended" when price has already run far above the Buy Zone or upper Bollinger band — still a fine trend, just a worse immediate entry; the two badges aren't mutually exclusive. Use the "Show: trend continuation" filter to see these on their own, and note this score is not blended into Setup Quality — same reasoning as Technical Confirmation above, it needs its own track record before it should move anything else.

Support / resistance. The main "Support" and "Resistance" columns are the simple version: the swing low of the current pullback leg, and the 6-month high — used consistently by the two scores below. Tap a row for Key Support/Resistance (1Y) — a more chart-like read using the trailing ~1 year of price history (252 trading days, sliced from the longer multi-year pull the backend keeps for the historical-analogue engine): it finds swing points (local lows/highs), groups nearby ones into a zone (shown as a range, e.g. $91–94, not a falsely-precise single number) and shows how many times that zone was tested (×N). A level touched 3 times over the past year is usually more meaningful than the current pullback's own low, and can sit well below or above it — check both rather than assuming the short one is the real line. Fix note: earlier versions of this tool ran that swing-point search across the entire multi-year GOOGLEFINANCE pull instead of just the trailing year, which could surface a "support zone" from 2–3 years back that a stock had long since left behind — e.g. a name now trading in the $200s still showing a zone around a $60 print from a couple of years ago. That's now bounded to the trailing year, so zones stay relevant to where the stock actually trades today.

Buy Zone / Stop / Target / R:R — now a ladder, not a single static pick. A heuristic trade framework built from the 1-year support/resistance zones above — not a signal or a recommendation, just a reference so the screen output turns into something actionable instead of a bare support number. The backend now returns up to 3 support zones below price, nearest-to-price first, and the frontend turns each into its own mini-framework: Buy Zone runs from about 2% above the bottom of that zone up to roughly half an ATR above that (capped at the zone's own top) — directly on top of Stop, not floated up near the zone's ceiling, so the entry band and the invalidation line stay tightly coupled instead of leaving the whole width of the tested cluster as an unexplained gap between them. The 2.2% lift off the raw zone low is a fixed, formulaic offset applied the same way to every zone on every ticker — not a per-stock adjustment — meant only to avoid anchoring the entry on the single lowest (and often noisiest) wick in the cluster. A single-touch (uncertain) zone has no real width of its own (its low and high are the same one touch), so it gets a small synthetic band instead — 0.05% of price by default, widened up to 0.15% when that would otherwise round to a sliver of only a few cents — so it stays a real, enterable range rather than one exact price tick. Stop sits about 0.75 ATR below the bottom of that zone — a level where the "this is still a pullback" thesis is probably wrong for that zone. Target walks up the resistance ladder (each zone's near edge, then the 6M high as a last resort) and picks the first level that clears the top of the Buy Zone by a real margin (~0.5 ATR) — not just "the nearest thing above price," which used to let a resistance zone sit inside or barely above the Buy Zone and produce an overlapping, meaningless range. If nothing on the ladder clears that margin — i.e. price has already broken above every known resistance level near it — Target shows a "breakout" badge instead of a fabricated number, and R:R is left blank rather than computed against a target that isn't really there. Each deeper zone targets the zone just above it under the same clearance rule, so the ladder reads as a set of stepped scale-in levels rather than one number. R:R is (Target − top of zone) / (top of zone − Stop), computed per zone. The table's Buy Zone column always shows the nearest zone (with a "+N" badge if deeper zones exist); tap a row to see the full ladder in the detail panel. If a stock has run well past all of its trailing-year support (little to no pullback yet), the nearest zone can still sit meaningfully below the current price — that's expected, not a bug: it's telling you where a real pullback would likely find buyers, not where price is right now. A symbol with no 1-year zone data at all (too little history) falls back to the simple swing-low/6M-high version — treat that single fallback zone as a rougher estimate. The backend prefers zones tested within the trailing ~3 months before reaching further back into the 1-year window, so the nearest zone reflects recent price action rather than a 6-12 month-old level a run-up has since left far behind — on a fast mover this is what keeps the zone from reading as unrealistically high. A zone touched only once (or the raw swing-low fallback) is tagged (uncertain) rather than presented the same as a level confirmed by 2+ separate tests — treat it as a rough reference, not a tested support level. The backend still prefers recent, well-tested zones over older or thinner ones when deciding which up-to-3 zones make the ladder, but the ladder itself — both the table's "+N" order and the detail panel — is always displayed nearest-to-price first, farthest last, regardless of which tier a zone came from.

Stale zone (new). A name can sit inside its own Buy Zone band for a week or two doing nothing — no breakout, no clean reversal, just parked — while every other score above stays green, since none of them track how long price has been there. The backend now counts nearestZoneDwellDays: consecutive trading days (most recent first) the close has stayed inside the nearest zone's Buy Zone band. Once that reaches 7 trading days (~1.5 weeks), the row is tagged stale Nd next to the Buy Zone (table and detail panel) and fails the Fresh Zone item on the Today's Top Picks checklist — so a stalled name drops out of the daily picks instead of getting recommended again day after day on an unchanged setup. It's still shown in the main table either way; this only affects the checklist count and the badge, not whether the row appears.

Support/resistance role reversal (new). A support level price has since broken below doesn't just disappear — classic chart reading treats it as new overhead resistance on the way back up (the buyers who bought there are often selling into it just to get back to breakeven), and symmetrically a broken resistance level becomes support on the next pullback. The backend now folds these flipped levels into the opposite zone list and tags them flipped; the frontend shows them as (former resistance) next to the Buy Zone and in the zone ladder. They're a real, useful level but a slightly less-proven claim than a zone that has always held in that direction, which is also why the Buy Zone buffer around a flipped zone is widened rather than treated identically (see next).

Recent, unconfirmed zones (new). Confirming a swing low/high requires ~2 weeks of price data on BOTH sides of it — you can't know a low was "the bottom" until price has moved away from it for a while. That's correct, but it means the most recent ~2 weeks are always invisible to the ladder by construction, and worse: a sharp new low in that stretch can retroactively disqualify an OLDER nearby point as a pivot (it's no longer the lowest point in ITS confirmation window) without being old enough to be confirmed itself — so right after a fresh selloff, the most relevant recent price action can vanish from the ladder entirely for about two weeks, while older, already-confirmed levels keep filling the 3 slots instead. The backend now adds one explicit placeholder per side: the single most extreme point in that unconfirmable trailing stretch, tagged (recent, unconfirmed) and always shown as a single touch. It ranks between confirmed-recent and confirmed-older zones (relevant given how fresh it is, but a rougher claim than an actually confirmed level) and gets a wider Buy Zone buffer than any confirmed zone, reflecting that lower confidence. It will not perfectly reconstruct every recent swing (e.g. a double-bottom that got invalidated this way still won't show as two touches until enough time passes) — it guarantees the single most recent extreme has SOME presence on the ladder rather than none.

Buy Zone width now reflects zone quality (changed). The buffer above a support zone used to be a flat 0.5×ATR no matter what. It's now scaled per zone: tighter (as low as ~0.63×ATR) for a zone touched 3+ times within the last ~3 months, wider (up to ~1.5×ATR) for a thin single-touch, older, role-reversed (flipped), or recent-unconfirmed (provisional) zone — a well-tested recent level genuinely pins price more precisely than a rough, old, reversed, or unconfirmed one, so the range you'd scale in across should be narrower for the former and wider for the latter. That scaling is now also capped at 2.5% of price regardless of how the quality math works out — on a high-ATR stock, the uncapped multiplier could still produce a single Buy Zone band spanning several percent of price, wide enough that price could move through most of it in one session without the band having told you much about where buyers actually are.

Overlapping zones now actually merge across support AND flipped levels (bug fix). A real, same-direction support cluster and a flipped former-resistance-turned-support cluster are built from two entirely separate pivot lists (lows vs. highs) — and a stock that broke out and pulled back to retest that breakout level is, by definition, sitting at BOTH a real support test and a former resistance level at the same time. The zone-merging pass only ever compared zones within the same list, so these two views of the same physical price never got compared against each other, and could show up as two separate, overlapping or near-identical "Zone 1" / "Zone 2" entries. The merge pass now runs again on the combined support+flipped list (same ATR-scaled overlap/gap rule as before), so this collapses into one zone.

Deepest Buy Level (new). Stop/Invalidation is specific to the nearest zone. Deepest Buy Level is the stop of the FARTHEST zone shown on the ladder instead — the final invalidation line if you were scaling in through every zone on the way down, not just the first one.

Sector-relative strength (new). The existing "vs S&P%" column only tells you a stock is beating the market — not whether that's real stock-specific strength or just a hot sector carrying it along. The backend now also computes vs Sector: this stock's 6-month return minus the average of its own sector's other tickers currently loaded (shown in the detail panel, alongside how many sector peers that average is based on). Needs at least 3 other same-sector tickers loaded to mean anything; otherwise it's left blank rather than computed on a sample of one or two. The Trend Continuation score now splits its relative-strength weight between the vs-SPY and vs-sector reads when both are available, instead of vs-SPY alone.

Pooled cross-ticker backtest (new). The Historical Analogues panel used to be self-referential only — one ticker's own ~3-year history, same caveats as before (small sample, single regime, no out-of-sample split). The backend now ALSO pools every ticker in today's loaded universe that hit the same pullback-band + trend combo at any point in its own history into one shared bucket, and the detail panel shows both: the pooled cross-ticker version first (bigger sample, closer to a real backtest, but still only as broad as your loaded ticker list and still a coarse band+trend match with no RSI/RS/volume awareness), then the original self-referential one below it for comparison. Pooled stats need 15+ matching occurrences across the universe before they're shown as a statistic rather than "too few, N=X."

Risk column. Two independent flags, shown together: Chasing measures how far price already sits above the top of the Buy Zone, in ATR units (≤0 ATR: not chasing; up to 1 ATR: moderate; beyond that: high — the setup has likely already run). Deep-Pullback confirmation only applies to the 30%+ band, since a severe decline is as likely to be something structurally broken as it is a buying opportunity. It counts how many of four reversal signals are present — price above/reclaiming the 50DMA, weekly trend up, relative strength vs SPY positive, and volume rising — and flags anything with one or none as a possible falling knife rather than a confirmed reversal.

Today's Top Picks checklist, restructured (changed). The checklist used to vote on 11 raw sub-metrics, several of which were partial restatements of each other — Setup Quality already folds in RSI, volume, and trend, and Trend Continuation already folds in long-term MA, ADX, and relative strength, so "RSI," "Long-Term Trend," and "High Volume" as separate items were partly double-counting signal already baked into those two composites. It's now 6 independent pillars, each worth one vote: the 3 composite scores the tool already keeps separate (Setup Quality, Trend Continuation, Tech Confirm), plus 3 merged raw-signal pillars — Momentum (MACD AND RSI must agree), Volume (relative volume AND accumulation must agree), and Entry Timing (in the Buy Zone, not extended, not stale — three checks on the same question, "is right now a good moment," collapsed into one vote instead of three).

Percentile rank + adaptive fallback (new). Fixed absolute thresholds (Setup Quality ≥70, etc.) read very differently depending on the tape — in a strong market almost every name clears them; in a weak one almost nothing does. Each candidate's blended composite score (average of Setup Quality / Trend Continuation / Tech Confirm, whichever are available) is now also ranked as a percentile against today's OTHER loaded candidates, shown on each card ("Nth pct. today"). If literally nothing clears the usual 60%-of-checklist bar on a given day, Top Picks falls back to showing the top percentile-rank name(s) instead of going empty — clearly labeled as a softer "relative leader" claim (best of what's here today), not a confirmed setup, so it's not confused with a normal pick.

Sector cap (new). Nothing used to stop all 3 daily picks coming from the same hot sector — which isn't 3 independent setups, it's one sector bet shown three times. Top Picks now allows at most 1 pick per sector by default, applied greedily in ranked order (a candidate is skipped, not the whole sector excluded up front, so one sector dominating the list doesn't accidentally empty it).

Test-Support % and Setup Quality % are heuristic model scores, not statistical probabilities. They're weighted composites of the signals below — a real historical win-rate is a separate thing, covered in its own entry further down, and deliberately kept separate rather than blended into these two scores:

  • Test-Support % weights proximity to support — measured in ATR (volatility) units rather than raw price range, so it's comparable across calm and jumpy stocks (30%) — against how far the pullback already ran (70%, capped around the 30%+ mark). Moved from an earlier 65/35 split after backtesting showed depth-of-pullback carries more signal than exact proximity to the low, holding up across repeated out-of-sample tests.
  • Setup Quality % blends four components: Pullback quality (45%) — recovery off the swing low (70% of this) and how recently that low was confirmed (30%); Geometry (30%) — room to run before hitting resistance, using a real touched resistance zone from the 1-year data if one sits closer than the simple 6-month high (60%), and proximity to support in ATR units (40%); Reversal confirmation (15%) — relative strength vs the S&P 500 (35% of this), RSI(14) (30%), volume trend (20%), and volume accumulation since the low (15%); and Trend (10%) — 20-day trend (34% of this), weekly trend as a higher-timeframe sanity check (33%), and long-term trend context (price vs 50D/200D moving averages, 33%). This weighting (moved from an earlier 30/20/35/15 trend/pullback/reversal/geometry split) held up across repeated out-of-sample backtests — it isn't a guess.
  • RSI(14) direction, Weekly Trend, and Volume-at-the-Low (tap a row) add further context. RSI catches a genuine momentum turn that a moving-average slope can miss — and the same RSI reading means something different climbing than falling, so the RSI column also shows direction (↑/↓/→ vs. ~5 trading days ago) and flags a fresh cross back above the 50 midline, which nudges the Setup Quality score slightly. Weekly Trend flags when the daily read might just be noise inside a bigger opposite move. Volume-at-the-Low shows whether the swing low happened on a real volume flush (often a stronger, more reliable base) or a quiet drift. Post-Low Volume (detail panel) is a related but separate read: since the low, is volume heavier on up-close days (Accumulation) or down-close days (Distribution)? None of these three volume/momentum reads are folded into the scores except the small RSI-direction nudge noted above — shown for your own judgment, since there's no solid basis here for exactly how much the rest should move a number one way or the other.
  • vs S&P% percentile. The vs S&P% column now also shows a percentile in parentheses, e.g. "+14% (96th)" — that's this stock's relative-strength rank against every other stock loaded today, not a fixed cutoff. A +4% return can be the best in a weak market or mediocre in a strong one; the percentile is what actually feeds the Setup Quality score (falls back to fixed thresholds only when there's nothing to rank against, e.g. a single manually-added row).
  • Liquidity. $Vol(20D) is 20-day average dollar volume (price × share volume) — a rough liquidity read. Nothing is excluded automatically; use the Liquidity filter above the table if you want to screen out names too thin to trade comfortably at size. Rows without volume data (manual entries, samples) aren't excluded by the filter since there's nothing to check them against.
  • Market Cap. Mkt Cap is the live GOOGLEFINANCE market cap, classified into mega ($200B+) / large ($10B+) / mid ($2B+) / small ($300M+) / micro (under $300M) bands. Use the Cap filter above the table to restrict to medium/large-cap names, or to isolate small/micro caps if that's what you're after. Today's Top Picks has no size/market-cap floor of its own — the best setup wins regardless of cap, and each pick's market cap is shown directly on its card so you can weigh that yourself rather than have it pre-filtered away.
  • Earnings. Earnings dates are intentionally not used by this version; GOOGLEFINANCE does not provide an earnings-date field.
  • Sector. GOOGLEFINANCE has no sector attribute either, so this comes from a built-in lookup table in Code.gs, with an optional Sector column in the Data sheet taking priority — anything not in that table shows —. The Sector filter above the table rebuilds itself from whatever sectors are actually present in what's loaded right now.

21D Win% (N) — historical analogues, and why this is NOT a real backtest. This is the one number in the whole tool built from actual historical outcomes rather than a weighted heuristic: for each stock, it scans that stock's own ~3-year price history for past points that would have been classified into the same pullback band + trend as today, and reports what happened over the following 21 trading days at each of those points — win rate, median/mean return, and the 25th–75th percentile spread (tap a row for the full breakdown). Read the caveats before leaning on it, though:

  • Single ticker only. Each stock's analogues come only from its own history — there's no pooling across similar setups on other names, so a stock that rarely pulls back this way will show a thin N (or none at all) even if the pattern is common across the market generally.
  • ~400 days is a short window. That's at most a cycle and a half. Every stat here is implicitly conditioned on whatever this particular stock's last ~18 months looked like — a stretch that might have been unusually calm, volatile, bullish, or bearish for that name specifically.
  • No out-of-sample validation. This reports hindsight statistics computed on the same window being screened — it hasn't been tested on data the model couldn't see, the way a real backtest would be.
  • The match is coarse — same pullback band and same trend state (up/down/flat) only. It doesn't require RSI, relative strength, volume, or anything else on this screen to also match, so two "analogous" points can still look quite different once you dig in.

Treat N below ~10 as closer to anecdote than statistic — the table flags anything under 5 as "thin" and doesn't compute stats at all below that. This exists to put a real (if narrow) number next to what the heuristic scores are guessing at, not to replace the cross-ticker, multi-year, leak-free backtesting infrastructure a genuinely robust version of this would need — that remains future work, not something this tool currently does.

Top 20 matches ranks the whole candidate pool by Setup Quality score first, then by 20-day liquidity as a tiebreaker, and shows the best 20 — this is the output of the screen, not a fixed watchlist. Click any column header to re-order that same top 20 by a different measure (e.g. tap "Cur DD %" to see the deepest current pullbacks among them); it doesn't change which 20 made the cut, only the order they're shown in.

Pagination. Every list is capped at 20 rows per page — "Show: top 20 matches" is inherently 20 or fewer so this never matters there, but "all loaded," "in any pullback band," and "pullback + momentum turning up" can easily run past 20 with a large ticker universe. When a filtered list is longer than one page, Prev/Next controls appear below the table; changing any filter, the search box, the depth band, or the sort column jumps back to page 1 of the newly-filtered list.

MA Trend checks price against the 50-day and 200-day moving averages (needs ~13 months of history, not just 6) — "Above 200D" means the pullback is happening within a longer-term uptrend; "Below 200D" means it's fighting that trend, which historically has a lower odds of a clean restart. vs S&P% is this stock's 6-month move minus SPY's over the same window — positive means it's outperforming the broad market, not just drifting with it. ATR Dist shows the distance to support/resistance in ATR (14-day volatility) units instead of raw price, so a $5 gap means something different for a $20 stock than a $500 one.

Use these to triage which names deserve a real look — a chart, volume profile, earnings calendar check — not as a signal to size a position off directly. This isn't financial advice.

"Fetch live data" calls a Google Apps Script Web App (Code.gs) deployed from your own Google Sheet — paste the URL it gives you (ending in /exec) into the field above. The Sheet's "Data" tab pulls ~3 years (1095 calendar days, resolving to roughly that many trading days) of daily prices per ticker via GOOGLEFINANCE — the 6-month slice drives the main pullback numbers, the trailing 1-year slice drives the Key Support/Resistance zones and Buy Zone ladder, and the full multi-year history feeds the 200-day MA and the 21D Win% historical-analogue engine — and the script reads that, computes everything, and returns it via JSONP — a script-tag loading trick that talks directly to Google with no proxy or relay involved, avoiding the CORS restrictions a plain fetch() runs into. Add "SPY" as one of the tickers for the vs-S&P-500 comparison to work. Optionally add a "NextEarnings" column to the Data tab (any date-parseable value, e.g. from your own earnings-calendar source) to populate the Earnings column — GOOGLEFINANCE has no earnings-date field, so this tool can't source it for you; leave the column out and Earnings just shows —. A large ticker list (hundreds of symbols) can take up to a minute for Apps Script to compute — that's normal, not a failure. If a fetch fails, it's almost always the deployment access setting (must be "Anyone" can access) or the Sheet still calculating — manual entry and "Load sample data" always work as a fallback.
