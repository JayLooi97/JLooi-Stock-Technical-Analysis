How to read this

This tool looks for stocks that dipped and then started recovering — a "pullback." It's a reference to help you think, not a buy/sell signal, and it's not financial advice.

  • Buy Zone — a price range where the stock has found support before. If the price falls back into this range, it may be a reasonable spot to consider buying.
  • Stop — if the price falls below this, the idea is probably wrong.
  • Target — a reasonable place to expect the price to run into resistance.
  • R:R (Reward:Risk) — how much you could gain (to Target) versus how much you're risking (to Stop). Higher is better.
  • Setup Quality score (0–100) — for stocks that have actually dipped: how good the pullback looks overall. Higher is better.
  • Trend Continuation score (0–100) — for stocks that are just trending up without a real dip. A separate way of looking at a stock, not blended with Setup Quality.
  • Test-Support score (0–100) — how close the price is to a real support level right now.
  • Colors: green = bullish / good sign, red = bearish / caution, amber = a warning worth noticing, violet = a rough or unconfirmed estimate — treat it with a bit less confidence.

Tap any row to see the full detail panel — support/resistance levels, the full buy-zone ladder, and every score broken down.

"Fetch live data" calls a Google Apps Script Web App (Code.gs) deployed from your own Google Sheet — paste the URL it gives you (ending in /exec) into the field above. The Sheet's "Data" tab pulls ~3 years (1095 calendar days, resolving to roughly that many trading days) of daily prices per ticker via GOOGLEFINANCE — the 6-month slice drives the main pullback numbers, the trailing 1-year slice drives the Key Support/Resistance zones and Buy Zone ladder, and the full multi-year history feeds the 200-day MA and the 21D Win% historical-analogue engine — and the script reads that, computes everything, and returns it via JSONP — a script-tag loading trick that talks directly to Google with no proxy or relay involved, avoiding the CORS restrictions a plain fetch() runs into. Add "SPY" as one of the tickers for the vs-S&P-500 comparison to work. Optionally add a "NextEarnings" column to the Data tab (any date-parseable value, e.g. from your own earnings-calendar source) to populate the Earnings column — GOOGLEFINANCE has no earnings-date field, so this tool can't source it for you; leave the column out and Earnings just shows —. A large ticker list (hundreds of symbols) can take up to a minute for Apps Script to compute — that's normal, not a failure. If a fetch fails, it's almost always the deployment access setting (must be "Anyone" can access) or the Sheet still calculating — manual entry and "Load sample data" always work as a fallback.
